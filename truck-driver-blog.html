Truck Driver Jobs in Europe

Essential guidelines for aspiring drivers from Asia seeking careers on European roads.

Overview

Europe offers a wealth of opportunities for qualified truck drivers. A modern fleet, reliable infrastructure and attractive salaries make long‑haul and regional driving roles appealing for non‑EU candidates. This article outlines what you need to know if you’re considering a career as a professional driver in Europe.

Why Work as a European Truck Driver?

  • Competitive pay: Drivers often earn higher wages than in many Asian countries, with additional allowances for overtime and international routes.
  • High safety standards: European regulations ensure well‑maintained vehicles, rest periods and road safety, reducing driver fatigue and accidents.
  • Stable employment: With ongoing driver shortages across Europe, qualified operators can secure long‑term contracts and steady income.
  • Cultural experience: Driving across borders exposes you to diverse landscapes, languages and cultures, making the job both professional and adventurous.

Core Requirements for Non‑EU Drivers

Before embarking on a European trucking career, non‑EU applicants must meet several legal and practical criteria. These typically include:

  • Heavy goods driver’s licence: A valid CE or C licence is mandatory. If obtained outside the EU, you may need to exchange it or take an EU driving test.
  • Code 95 (CPC) certificate: This qualification demonstrates your knowledge of EU road safety and professional driver standards. It is often required in conjunction with the licence.
  • Tachograph card: Drivers must record their hours and rest periods using a digital tachograph. Applying for a tachograph card is required in most EU countries.
  • Job offer and work permit: Employers typically sponsor work permits and must demonstrate a genuine need for international staff. Once the permit is approved, you can apply for a national visa or EU Blue Card.
  • Visa and residency: A long‑stay visa or residence permit is needed to legally live and work in the host country. Requirements vary but often include proof of accommodation, financial means and clean criminal records.
  • Medical and background checks: Many employers request medical fitness, psychotechnical assessments and criminal record certificates.
  • Language skills: Basic knowledge of the host country’s language (German, Dutch, etc.) helps with road signs, communication and integration.

Country Guides

Below are highlights for five popular European destinations. Always consult official government sources and employers for the most up‑to‑date requirements.

Germany

Europe’s largest logistics market has a persistent driver shortage, welcoming skilled operators from abroad.

  • Average salary: €2 200–€2 800 per month plus allowances.
  • Employers assist with work permit (Arbeitserlaubnis) and EU residence visa.
  • Code 95 and tachograph card training can be completed locally.
  • Basic German language proficiency is advisable for communication and safety.

Netherlands

The Dutch logistics sector relies heavily on international drivers to keep trade flowing.

  • Average salary: €2 300–€3 000 per month with bonuses for international routes.
  • Employers handle IND work permits and assist with BSN registration.
  • Some companies arrange language courses and housing for newcomers.
  • Familiarity with Dutch road rules and tachograph use is essential.

Belgium

Strategically located near major ports, Belgium offers numerous regional and cross‑border routes.

  • Average salary: €2 100–€2 700 per month.
  • Work permit applications are processed via regional authorities (Vlaanderen/Wallonie).
  • French or Dutch language skills are advantageous depending on region.
  • Dual‑use vehicles (international and domestic) are common.

Romania

Romania serves as a hub for Eastern European logistics and employs drivers for both domestic and EU routes.

  • Average salary: €1 200–€1 800 per month with per‑diem allowances.
  • Foreign drivers need a residence permit and can exchange non‑EU licences.
  • Less stringent language requirements, but knowledge of Romanian helps daily life.
  • Growing demand for long‑haul drivers to Western Europe.

Slovakia

Strategically located in Central Europe, Slovakia offers access to both Eastern and Western corridors.

  • Average salary: €1 400–€2 200 per month.
  • Employers sponsor work permits; EU residence permits allow travel within the Schengen area.
  • Training for Code 95 and tachograph card can be completed after arrival.
  • Slovak language basics are helpful but not always mandatory for international routes.
